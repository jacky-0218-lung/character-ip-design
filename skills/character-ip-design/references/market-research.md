# Market Research Reference（市場調查：先看清戰場，再畫第一筆）

Read this at Phase 1 Step 2, before any design work on a full-pipeline IP brief. Professional
IP is goal-first, not art-first: Kumamon was commissioned as a tourism instrument before it
was a bear; POP MART signs artists before products exist; San-X positions "relaxed, powerless"
healing characters deliberately AGAINST Sanrio's polished kawaii. The report you produce here
decides the white space the design will occupy.

## Scale it

- **Full IP brief** (user wants an IP business: stickers→merch→licensing, or says 市場調查):
  full protocol below, 8–15 searches, report of 800–1500 words with sources.
- **Quick mascot brief** (a shop wants a mascot): compress to a competitor scan + a name/motif
  collision check（撞名/撞motif；與 verification-tests 的剪影撞形 collision 是不同的檢查），
  3–5 searches, half a page inside the design sheet's 定位摘要. Don't inflict a 40-page bible
  on a coffee shop.
- No web access → say so, use built-in knowledge, mark every claim as unverified.

## Protocol（fixed order）

### 1. Category & market sizing（1–2 searches）

One paragraph of context with real numbers when available. Useful anchors (verify/update by
search; cite what you use): global licensed merch ~$390B (Licensing International); Japan
character business ~¥2.85T (Yano Research); China licensed merch ~RMB 155B (China Licensing
Expo white paper); LINE Creators Market has shown solo creators can reach ¥100M+ (LINE PR).
Pick the numbers relevant to the user's market and goals.

### 2. Competitor IP landscape & motif occupancy（3–5 searches）

The core artifact: a table of the 6–12 IPs the target audience already loves in this
category/market. Columns: IP name / motif (animal/object) / style register / super symbol /
persona doctrine / primary surfaces / price tier. Then read the table for **occupancy**:
which motifs are taken (cute cat in TW stickers: crowded; 台灣雲豹: open), which style
registers are saturated, which persona registers (healing 治癒 / deadpan / hyper-genki) are
crowded. Industry precedent: Yano's reports literally map the "cute" category as occupied
territory (Chiikawa + Sanrio); occupancy analysis is standard practice, not paranoia.

### 3. Audience insight（1–3 searches）

Who actually buys: segment (e.g., 20–40 女性上班族 character-goods buyers, kidult collectors,
students), where they hang out (LINE/IG/小紅書/Threads/市集), what they spend on (stickers,
gacha, plush, 文創), and the emotional job the IP must do (治癒/共感/身分表達/收藏慾). The
wallet is real; the question is which emotion opens it.

### 4. Positioning white space（no search — synthesis）

One statement: "In [market], for [audience], every incumbent offers [occupied registers];
the open position is [motif × register × style] because [evidence from the table]." This
sentence is the brief for the design phase — the direction fan-out must aim at it.

### 5. Trademark & name quick screen（1–2 searches; NOT legal advice）

Before falling in love with a name: search the name + 商標 in target markets (TIPO 台灣 /
J-PlatPat 日本 / CNIPA 中國 / USPTO / WIPO Global Brand DB), plus social handles and domains.
Relevant Nice classes for character goods (non-exhaustive — ALWAYS add the brand's own primary
class too, e.g. 3 soap/cosmetics, 30 food, 43 café): 9 (digital stickers), 16 (stationery),
18 (bags), 25 (apparel), 28 (toys/plush), 35 (retail), 41 (entertainment). Note POP MART files
multi-jurisdiction with priority claims and Kumamon got squatted overseas — recommend early
filing in target markets. Always label this section「初步檢索，非法律意見，正式申請前請洽商標
師」.

### 6. Cultural screen（when the IP targets more than one market）

Check the chosen motif/colors/name across target markets: white = funeral contexts in East
Asia; purple = mourning in Thailand/Brazil; owl = ill omen in parts of India/Middle East;
pig motifs sensitive in Muslim markets; 綠帽 in China; avoid 4-counts in JP/CN/KR SKU and
pricing; back-translate the name in EN/JP/ZH/KO.

**Cite with live links, not just names.** Every sourced number and every competitor claim gets
a real hyperlinked URL in the report (markdown `[來源](https://…)`), not a bare publication
name — an unlinked "據 Yano 研究" is not verifiable by the client and reads as hand-waving.
When a fetch fails, say what you couldn't verify rather than citing it unlinked.

## Output: 市場分析報告 template

```
# 市場分析報告：[working title]
## 市場概況        （sizing + trend, 1 段, 附連結來源）
## 競品 IP 地圖    （the occupancy table + 2–3 句 reading）
## 受眾洞察        （segment, channels, spend, emotional job）
## 空白定位        （the white-space statement — 這句話就是設計 brief）
## 命名與商標初檢   （candidates screened, classes, 風險註記, 非法律意見聲明）
## 文化檢核        （if multi-market）
## 來源            （linked）
```

The white-space statement feeds Phase 2 Step 4 directly: all three concept directions must
be defensible answers to it, and the design sheet's positioning section quotes it verbatim.
