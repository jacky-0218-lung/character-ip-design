# Super Symbol Reference（超級符號詳解）

Read this before committing to an amplification axis (Step 6) or when a draft feels weak.

## What "amplification" actually means

Amplification = pushing one local feature far past natural proportion, until it becomes the
character's name in people's heads ("the one with the huge ears"). Four techniques, roughly in
order of power:

1. **Scale（放大）** — 1.5×–2× beyond natural proportion. Below ~1.3× reads as "slightly big",
   which is the worst zone: noticeable but not memorable. Cinnamoroll's ears are wider than
   his body — that is the level of commitment required.
2. **Absence（缺席）** — removing a feature everyone expects. Hello Kitty has no mouth; Miffy's
   mouth is a tiny ×. Absence is powerful because the viewer's brain keeps noticing the gap.
3. **Repetition（重複）** — one shape repeated rhythmically. Labubu's nine serrated teeth work
   because they are *many and identical*; three irregular teeth would just look damaged.
4. **Geometric purity（幾何化）** — reducing the feature (or whole body) to a near-perfect
   primitive. Totoro is one huge egg; Baymax is stacked capsules. Purity makes the character
   redrawable by anyone — which is exactly what "infinitely extensible" requires.

## Case analyses by axis

### Facial-feature axis（五官）

- **Labubu** — nine identical serrated teeth in a wide grin. Everything else (fur, body) is
  generic plush vocabulary; the teeth do 100% of the identification work. Note the repetition
  technique.
- **Hello Kitty** — the missing mouth (absence), supported by a yellow button nose. Sanrio's
  official rationale — "so she mirrors your own mood" — shows a symbol can carry meaning, not
  just recognition.
- **Jellycat vegetables** — two bean-dot eyes + a 2 px smile on *any* object. Proof that a
  facial symbol can be a portable system: the same face turns an eggplant or a croissant into
  the same brand.
- **Pikachu** — red circular cheeks. The yellow body is loud, but the cheeks are the
  identifier — cover them and Pikachu becomes "a yellow mouse".

When to choose: sticker packs, chat/reaction use, in-app guide characters, anything where the
character must emote constantly. A facial symbol survives expression changes by definition —
the other features flex around it.

### Form axis（形態）

- **Cinnamoroll** — ears ≈ 1.2× head width, shaped like wings (and canonically used to fly:
  the symbol generates story, the story never gets welded onto the body).
- **Totoro** — the entire body is one massive egg; belly chevrons are the only decoration.
  Reads perfectly at any size and from any angle.
- **Baymax** — capsule stack + two dot-eyes-one-line face. An industrial designer's symbol:
  the *softness of the volume* is the amplified feature.
- **Standard cute construction** — 2–3 頭身 (head-to-body ratio). 2 頭身 = maximum萌, best for
  stickers and plush; 3 頭身 = more limb freedom for poses and animation; 4+ 頭身 drifts toward
  fashion/idol IP and out of "cute mascot" territory.

When to choose: plush toys, standees, physical merch, silhouette-first contexts (event
backdrops, keychains). Form symbols are the most silhouette-robust — they *are* the silhouette.

### Color axis（色彩）

- **Duolingo's Duo** — a green owl. The green is the brand; the owl shape is deliberately
  simple so the color can own it.
- **Minions** — signature yellow + goggles. Yellow does the shelf-distance work; goggles do the
  close-range work. (Note: this is one *color* symbol with a facial support, not two symbols —
  the support never competes in scale.)
- **Kumamon** — a *black* bear with red cheeks, when every other cute mascot was pastel.
  Choosing the counterintuitive hue for the category is half the technique.

When to choose: the brand already owns a color; the category shelf is crowded; the primary
surface is an app icon or avatar at small sizes. A color symbol needs discipline: one dominant
hue ≥ ~70% of the character's area, support colors kept neutral. **This 70% rule is for
color-axis characters ONLY** — facial- and form-axis characters follow the 4-role palette
floor in `appeal.md` instead; letting this rule leak into other axes is how mascots end up
90% one mid-tone and dead.

## Choosing when applications conflict

Real briefs mix applications (stickers *and* plush). Resolve by ranking: which surface earns
the brand money or attention *first*? Design the symbol for that surface, then verify it
survives the others in the extension test. A facial symbol on a plush is fine (Labubu is a
plush empire); a form symbol in stickers is fine (Cinnamoroll stickers exist) — the axis
choice is about where the symbol works *hardest*, not where it works *only*.

If the brand has a mandatory color asset but the application says facial/form: use the brand
color as the character's base coat (that is livery, not a symbol) and amplify on the
application's axis. State this distinction in the design sheet so the client understands the
color is inherited, not the identity.

## Shape language quick reference

The un-amplified remainder of the character should be generic *in shape* — simple, redrawable
geometry — but never lifeless: the appeal pass in `appeal.md` (palette floor, statue check,
detail budget, persona) is what keeps "quiet" from becoming "empty". Choose the base geometry
by personality keyword:

- Circles / ovals — friendly, safe, approachable (default for cute IP)
- Squares / rounded rectangles — stable, reliable, sturdy (good for security, finance, tools)
- Triangles / diagonals — energy, speed, mischief (use sparingly; sharp = less huggable)

A security-company IP can be a rounded-square body (reliable) with one amplified feature; the
shape language whispers the personality while the symbol shouts the identity.

## Warning signs the symbol is too weak

- You describe the character with a list ("a bear with a scarf and glasses and…") instead of
  one clause ("the bear with the enormous round glasses").
- The silhouette test needs the color version alongside to be recognized.
- Two features keep fighting for the viewer's first glance.
- The feature disappears in profile or at 64 px.

Any of these → return to subtraction and either amplify harder (scale up, purify geometry) or
subtract more around the symbol.
